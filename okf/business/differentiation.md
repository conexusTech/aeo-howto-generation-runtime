# Differentiation

## The consequence being managed is index filtering, not a penalty

The instinct is that duplicate content across domains draws a ranking penalty.
It rarely does. What happens instead is quieter and worse: one shop's article is
indexed and the others are **suppressed as duplicates**, so fifty clients
quietly become one. Retrieval converges the same way, which gives an answer
engine no reason to cite any particular copy.

Every article is live. Every article is correct. All but one are invisible, and
nothing in the product reports this.

## Why variable substitution is not differentiation

A template with the shop's name, city and services substituted in produces fifty
documents that are ninety-something percent identical. That is precisely the
shape a deduplicating index collapses. Differentiation has to reach **structure,
ordering, emphasis and examples** — which is why generation is a model call
against the shop's own record rather than a rendering step.

## The check is a human, and that is settled

HOW-4.3's criterion is that ten shops generated from one template produce
articles a human reviewer judges to read as distinct pieces. ⚠️ **This is
deliberately subjective and cannot be automated in V1.** That is the settled
position, not an oversight — and the flag exists so the limit is not quietly
"fixed" into an automated gate that reports green while measuring nothing.

[Similarity measurement](/lib/similarity.md) stores a number alongside the
judgement so it becomes auditable, but the number is evidence, not a verdict. A
low lexical score on two articles that read identically is entirely possible;
that is what the reviewer is for.

⚠️ **Nobody is assigned to be that reviewer.** Recorded on the workspace roadmap
as an open dependency. Until somebody is, HOW-4.3 has no exit criterion that can
be met.
